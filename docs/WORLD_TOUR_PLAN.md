# Pocket Gacha: World Atlas plan

This document extends `ROADMAP.md`. It consolidates the expandable content,
roster, gacha and difficulty structure without replacing the immediate goal:
make one polished five-stage roaming-combat slice before producing hundreds of
characters.

The plan is intentionally expandable. Numbers here describe good production
sizes, not lifetime caps.

## 1. Content hierarchy

The game has five content layers:

1. **Library** — the whole game and account-wide collection.
2. **Shelf** — a browsing filter, not a combat rule or progression gate.
3. **Saga** — one franchise or coherent anthology, such as Mario or Arcade Hall.
4. **World** — a campaign or destination inside a Saga.
5. **Area** — a five-stage environment and its concluding boss.

Example: `Library > Mario Saga > Mushroom Kingdom > Haunted House > Stage 13`.

A World can contain 5, 10, 15, 20 or 25 story stages. Twenty-five is the
preferred size for a major flagship campaign such as Mushroom Kingdom or Dream
Land, not a rule that should stretch small ideas or cut large ones short. Every
five-stage Area provides a complete visual/mechanical arc and a boss.

There is no 100-stage cap on a Saga. Mario can keep receiving new destinations
as long as they support worthwhile enemies, characters and mechanics. Mastery
is a separate difficulty track for completed content rather than stages 26–100
being appended mechanically to every World.

### Shelves

Shelves keep a very large Library readable. A Saga may appear under more than
one filter.

- **Action and platform:** Mario, Kirby, Mega Man, Sonic, Metroid,
  Castlevania, Doom and Hollow Knight.
- **Adventure and RPG:** Zelda, Dragon Quest, Final Fantasy, Pokemon, Fire
  Emblem and EarthBound.
- **Arcade Hall:** Pac-Man, Donkey Kong, Dig Dug, Galaga and other games best
  represented as short cabinets inside one anthology Saga.
- **Fighting Circuit:** Street Fighter first, with room for other fighting
  games whose sheets are especially good for roaming combat.
- **Modern shelf:** Minecraft, Terraria, Hollow Knight, Henry Stickmin,
  Among Us and Bloons TD6.
- **Oddities drawer:** meme, live-action and deliberately strange crossover
  batches after the main game is established.

Shelves do not create type bonuses, shared banners or mandatory play order.

## 2. Player choice versus production order

The team still needs a production order so assets and systems can be built
coherently. The player does not need to follow that same order.

Mushroom Kingdom is the initial tutorial World. Finishing its final boss awards
an **Atlas Key** and reveals a small group of eligible destinations. A key opens
one destination now; the others remain available for later keys. Nothing is
permanently lost and every unlocked World can be revisited.

The Atlas should normally show two to four sensible choices, not the entire
future catalog. It can mark one recommended route without requiring it.
Prerequisites are used only where a campaign depends on a character, mechanic
or earlier story event.

An example early choice could be:

- Dream Land — begin a new Saga.
- Koopalings' Grand Tour — continue Mario's main conflict.
- Luigi's Mansion — take a mechanically different Mario side route.

Finishing whichever one the player chooses earns another key. The unchosen
routes remain on the Atlas.

### Working production sequence

The planned build sequence remains narrower than the eventual player map:

1. Mushroom Grasslands stages 1–5.
2. Mushroom Kingdom stages 1–25.
3. Dream Land stages 1–25.
4. The strongest sprite-ready choice among Koopalings, Mega Man and Sonic.
5. Expand according to sprite readiness and playtest interest.

This is a development sequence, not a permanent campaign railroad.

## 3. Franchise production groups

Sprite availability is not the same as animation suitability. The following
groups are starting estimates and can change after an asset audit.

- **Group A, naturally suited:** Mario, Kirby, Mega Man and Sonic.
- **Group B, strong combat sheets:** fighting games, Castlevania, Metroid,
  Doom and Arcade Hall.
- **Group C, plentiful but adaptation-heavy:** Zelda, Pokemon, Final Fantasy,
  Dragon Quest, Fire Emblem and EarthBound.
- **Group D, custom or modern treatment:** Minecraft, Terraria, Hollow Knight,
  Henry Stickmin, Among Us, Bloons TD6 and Oddities.

Group C games often have abundant sprites but not the running, attacking and
hit reactions that the desktop strip needs. They may require reconstructed
animation and stronger effect work.

## 4. First two flagship Worlds

The first planned content batch remains two 25-stage anchors. This proves both
depth within a World and variety between Sagas without making 25 a universal
length.

### Mushroom Kingdom

| Stages | Area | Enemies introduced | Boss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–5 | Mushroom Grasslands | Goomba, Koopa, Piranha Plant, Paratroopa | Goomba King |
| 6–10 | Underground Mines | Swooper, Buzzy Beetle, Spike, Bob-omb | King Bob-omb |
| 11–15 | Haunted House | Boo, Dry Bones, Peepa, Big Boo | King Boo |
| 16–20 | Koopa Airship | Bullet Bill, Lakitu, Spiny, Hammer Bro, Magikoopa | Bowser Jr. |
| 21–25 | Bowser's Castle | Lava Bubble, Thwomp, elite Koopa troops | Bowser |

Each enemy needs one readable mechanical identity, not merely different art.
Bosses need behavior that reflects the character:

- Goomba King commands adds and creates body-slam shockwaves.
- King Bob-omb leaves explosives that either side can trigger.
- King Boo uses false copies, vanishing and backline pressure.
- Bowser Jr. changes altitude and uses Clown Car hazards.
- Bowser breaks safe ground and changes moves in phase two.

### Dream Land

| Stages | Area | Enemy direction | Boss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–5 | Green Greens | Waddle Dee, Bronto Burt, Sir Kibble, Poppy Bros. Jr. | Whispy Woods |
| 6–10 | Castle Dedede | Waddle Doo, Broom Hatter, Rocky, Bonkers | King Dedede |
| 11–15 | Cloudy Park | Parasol units, Scarfy, Birdon and storm hazards | Kracko |
| 16–20 | Halberd | Axe Knight, Mace Knight and mechanical enemies | Meta Knight |
| 21–25 | Fountain of Dreams | mirror and nightmare variants | Nightmare |

Dream Land must not be a Mario reskin. Kirby copy forms, flying enemies and
dream-state hazards need their own movement and presentation.

## 5. Mario destination map, without a stage cap

Mushroom Kingdom gives Mario a complete opening and defeats Bowser at stage 25.
Later destinations are expansions, not extra filler before that conclusion.
The following lengths are design targets, not promises to pad or cut content.

| World | Target length | Content direction |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Mushroom Kingdom | 25 | Grasslands through Bowser's Castle; introductory anchor |
| Koopalings' Grand Tour | 15–20 | Distinct regions, paired Koopaling fights, Dry Bowser finale |
| Luigi's Mansion | 15–25 | Ghost control, darkness, traps, E. Gadd and Ascendant King Boo |
| Isle Delfino | 10–15 | FLUDD hazards, Piantas, Shadow Mario and Petey Piranha |
| Sprixie Kingdom | 10 | 3D World enemies, Cat forms and Meowser |
| Galaxy | 20–25 | gravity, Lumas, planetoids and Cosmic Bowser |
| Odyssey | 20–25 | kingdoms, captures, Broodals and Cappy Mario |
| Lake Lapcat | 10 | Fury hazards, giant-scale encounters and Fury Bowser |

Additional Mario Worlds can be added when they justify a cast, enemy set or
mechanic. The Atlas stores progress per World, so adding one does not require
renumbering everything that came before it.

## 6. Difficulty and scaling

The game must avoid both exponential stat walls and a world map where every
side route becomes trivial after one clear.

### Rules

- The account has an **Atlas Rank** that rises at major final-boss milestones.
- Every World declares a minimum and maximum **Threat Band** plus a recommended
  party power range.
- When the player first enters a new World, its Story Threat is set from the
  current Atlas Rank within that World's supported band. It does not keep
  moving upward during the campaign.
- Enemy strength comes from an authored encounter budget, not one endless
  exponential global stage formula.
- The game never scales directly to the exact party. Pulling, leveling and
  building a better team must still create a real advantage.
- An older route may eventually become easy, which is acceptable for farming.
  Mastery reopens it at the current Atlas Rank with modifiers and improved
  bosses when the player wants a challenge.
- Clearing a sibling route may make another route somewhat easier through a
  better collection, but should not make its authored mechanics irrelevant.
- An optional higher Threat setting can be selected when entering a World for
  better first-clear rewards; difficulty cannot be lowered after rewards from
  that setting are claimed.

### Reference-team testing

Every stage is tested against several saved reference teams:

- **Minimum:** the free/story characters and expected upgrade floor.
- **Typical:** a mixed R/SR party with ordinary pulls.
- **Premium:** SSR-heavy, but without assuming a particular limited unit.
- **UR check:** verifies that one UR feels powerful without deleting the boss.

No normal story stage requires a particular pull. Traits such as Fire,
Airborne, Ghost, Armored or Explosive can create meaningful advantages, but
they do not become keys that make a boss literally unbeatable without one
unit. A specialized SR can outperform a general SSR when its traits answer the
encounter.

Bosses use phase gates, telegraphs and mechanic changes rather than only giant
health totals. This lets an SSR or UR feel much faster and stronger without one
transformation erasing every boss before it demonstrates its identity.

Threat numbers, recommended power ranges and stat curves remain open until the
first five stages can be repeatedly simulated and played.

## 7. Rarity philosophy

Rarity should feel like a real upgrade. The design no longer requires R units
to remain almost equal to SSR units at endgame.

- **R:** the introductory/base version of a character. Cheap, available early
  and expected to be replaced eventually within that family.
- **SR:** a strong specialist, alternate iteration, lieutenant or miniboss.
  The right SR can beat an SSR in a favorable encounter.
- **SSR:** a major general upgrade, signature interpretation or landmark boss.
  Pulling one should noticeably change the party.
- **UR:** a peak, historic or deliberately overpowered interpretation. UR is
  not synonymous with transformation, though some UR ultimates transform.

The exact numerical gaps are deliberately not locked before combat testing.
The intended ordering is clear: R is an introduction, SR is competitive through
specialization, SSR is broadly stronger, and UR is the exceptional team anchor.

R units can still have uses in rarity-restricted challenges, collection tasks
and early progression, but the balance does not contort itself to make base
Mario as valuable as an endgame Mario forever.

R pulls must still return useful account value after their fighter is retired.
Duplicates raise the existing Ascension track; duplicates beyond its cap turn
into Archive currency that can buy Supply Tickets, upgrade materials or
long-term selector fragments. The exact shop economy waits for pull-rate tests.

The party has one **Mythic slot** across its entire loadout. It may be occupied
by one UR fighter, one UR Power-Up or one future UR Assist Page. That
restriction is intentionally severe: the player chooses which exceptional
identity the team is built around. UR options therefore need distinct attack
behaviors, roles and encounter traits rather than all being interchangeable
damage dealers.

## 8. Fighter iterations versus Power-Ups

Creating Fire, Ice, Tanooki, Cat and Propeller versions as separate fighters
would flood the roster with Marios and multiply the art/sprite workload. The
consolidated system separates **fighter iterations** from **Power-Ups**.

### Fighter iterations

A separate fighter must represent a meaningful era, identity or complete
moveset. Candidate Mario iterations include:

- Base/Classic Mario as the introductory R.
- A 64 or retro interpretation built around jumps, punches and throws.
- Sunshine Mario built around FLUDD.
- Galaxy Mario built around spin and Luma interactions.
- Cappy Mario built around throws and captures.
- Paper Mario or Dr. Mario when their sprite style and kits justify a full unit.

Rarity is decided from the kit and release context. Retro Mario is not
automatically UR merely because he is historically important; Cappy Mario is
not automatically UR merely because he is modern. Those remain roster choices
after the sprite and kit audit.

### Equippable Power-Ups

A fighter can equip one compatible Power-Up. A Power-Up adds a trait, passive,
move replacement or ultimate modifier and may temporarily change the sprite.

Examples:

- Fire Flower adds the Fire trait and a projectile or burn interaction.
- Ice Flower adds control and can freeze compatible hazards.
- Tanooki Leaf adds air time, tail attacks or evasion.
- Super Bell adds wall movement and pounce behavior.
- Propeller Mushroom adds vertical evasion and a slam.
- Super Star grants a short invulnerable contact-damage state.

The Super Star should not grant a literal full minute of invincibility in a
one-to-three-minute stage. Its duration is tuned to create a dominant window,
probably 8–20 seconds depending on rarity and activation condition.

Power-Ups reduce duplicate fighter production while increasing team building:
Sunshine Mario with a Fire Flower and Sunshine Mario with a Tanooki Leaf can
answer different encounters without becoming two separately leveled Marios.

Some iconic forms can still be fighters when the transformation defines the
entire kit. Fury Bowser is a valid UR fighter; a generic Fire Mario probably
works better as Mario plus a Fire Flower.

Power-Up compatibility must not recreate the same asset problem as separate
fighters. A Power-Up has one universal mechanical/VFX implementation, a list
of compatible fighter tags and optional bespoke sprite frames for priority
combinations. Unsupported combinations are blocked; supported combinations do
not each require a complete new sprite sheet. The first slice implements only
a few authored combinations before attempting a franchise-wide equipment
matrix.

### Candidate Mario roster

This is a candidate pool, not a launch promise. Characters enter production
only with a relevant World and usable animation plan.

- **Usually base/R:** Mario, Luigi, Peach, Daisy, Toad, Toadette, Yoshi,
  Birdo, Wario, Waluigi, Rosalina and Pauline.
- **Usually SR specialists or lieutenants:** Bowser Jr., Kamek, Petey
  Piranha, Boom Boom, Pom Pom and the seven Koopalings.
- **SSR boss/signature candidates:** Bowser, King Bob-omb, King Boo, Dry
  Bowser, Boolossus, Hellen Gravely, Meowser and major era-specific heroes.
- **UR/Mythic candidates:** Fury Bowser, Cappy Mario, a Galaxy-scale Mario or
  Rosalina interpretation, and other peak forms that justify the slot.

Lore importance does not automatically assign rarity. Base Rosalina may still
begin above R if her smallest honest kit is already mechanically exceptional;
Retro Mario is not automatically UR simply because he is historically iconic.
The sprite audit and kit fantasy decide the final entry.

## 9. Yoshi and small support characters

Adult Yoshi colors can be separate fighter iterations when their behavior is
genuinely different:

- Red Yoshi emphasizes fire and damage over time.
- Blue Yoshi emphasizes flight and aerial control.
- Yellow Yoshi emphasizes ground slams and shockwaves.

They share the Yoshi family lock. Palette-only variations are not separate
units.

Baby Yoshis are better candidates for Assist Pages or Power-Up-style helpers.
They can appear in art, provide a passive, or briefly intervene without needing
a complete roaming combat sheet.

## 10. Assist Pages and supply collectibles

The collectible system has three distinct categories:

1. **Fighters** — occupy party slots and require full battle animation.
2. **Power-Ups** — equip to compatible fighters and modify their kits.
3. **Assist Pages** — one party-wide illustrated memory, character or scene
   providing a passive and one charged/conditional intervention.

Assist candidates include E. Gadd's Laboratory, Toadsworth's Advice, Luma's
Guidance, Peach's Tea Time, Cappy's Intervention, FLUDD Emergency Spray,
Polterpup and baby Yoshis.

Power-Ups and Assist Pages share a cheaper **Supply** gacha. The Supply gacha
uses Supply Tickets earned from stages, missions and duplicates rather than the
premium currency used for fighters. This solves the weapon-banner problem:
players do not have to sacrifice character savings merely to engage with the
support system.

Supply rarity can reach UR, but the party's single Mythic slot still applies.
Equipping an UR Super Star therefore prevents deploying Fury Bowser at the same
time. This turns the restriction into a real build choice instead of allowing
several supposedly exceptional effects to stack.

The permanent Supply Archive is always available. Each active Area can provide
a temporary Supply Spotlight with better rates for relevant Pages and
Power-Ups. When the Spotlight ends, its contents enter the permanent Archive
at normal rates; nothing becomes unobtainable.

The first implementation uses one Assist Page for the whole party and one
Power-Up slot per fighter. More equipment slots are deferred until this version
proves understandable.

## 11. Banner lifecycle

### Fighter banners

- A World's banner unlocks with the World.
- Area Spotlights open as their content is reached.
- A defeated boss can enter the playable pool after its first defeat.
- The current Area receives boosted rates during play and for a generous grace
  period measured in active play, not while the application is closed.
- When a Spotlight ends, its fighters move into that Saga's permanent Archive
  at normal rates and can receive future reruns.

This gives the active adventure excitement without permanently deleting
characters or making the Archive worthless. The first implementation may use
progress-based Spotlights instead of a clock: the feature lasts until the next
World finale. Real-time expiration is deferred until it proves more fun than
annoying in a personal desktop game.

### Supply banners

- Cheaper than fighter banners.
- Use separately earned Supply Tickets.
- Contain Power-Ups and Assist Pages, never fighters.
- Area Spotlights rotate into one permanent Supply Archive.

### UR banners

- Separate from ordinary fighter banners with their own persistent pity.
- Unlock candidates through boss defeats, achievements or Saga progress.
- Rate-up rotations can cycle among unlocked URs.
- Previously unlocked URs remain in the Mythic Archive even when not featured.
- Major long-term achievements should eventually award a selectable UR so the
  system is not pure luck.

The current 3% SSR, soft pity at 50 and hard pity at 70 remain the ordinary
fighter baseline to playtest. Supply costs, UR rates, grace periods and pity
are intentionally open until real earn rates exist.

## 12. Ultimates and transformations

Every fighter can have an ultimate. Transforming is one ultimate category, not
the definition of UR.

- Fury Bowser can leap away, smash back at giant scale, gain a new flame and
  shockwave move set, then return to normal.
- An Invincible Mario iteration could activate a Super Star and become a
  high-speed contact-damage unit for a limited window.
- A different UR may remain in one form and instead provide an extraordinary
  summon, support field, screen-wide attack or control effect.

Transformations target roughly 12–20 seconds, adjusted to stage length. Large
forms use the strip's background plane and camera effects so they feel enormous
without turning the transparent desktop strip into an obstructive solid panel.

## 13. Asset database and future scraping

Every fighter receives an animation-readiness grade before production:

- **A:** movement, attack, hit and defeat animation are ready.
- **B:** strong base sheet; needs effects or a few reconstructed frames.
- **C:** mostly static; requires substantial custom animation.
- **D:** no suitable source; requires original sprite work.

The asset registry should record:

- franchise, game, era and character/form tags;
- source page and original file URL;
- sheet dimensions, frame regions, directions and animation names;
- missing actions and readiness grade;
- duplicate hashes, attribution and usage notes;
- processed in-game assets derived from the source.

If authenticated accounts are connected later, a large authorized scrape can
feed this registry. Credentials must be connected through the environment's
secret/account system rather than pasted into chat or committed. The scraper
should store source metadata and resumable manifests so finding assets and
processing them are separate operations.

The database is a future production multiplier, not a prerequisite for the
first playable slice. The Spriters Resource currently requires the owner to
download sheets manually into `incoming/`; the importer can already be planned
around that same registry shape.

Prototype assets can prove the private game loop. A public build would need
original, licensed or commissioned replacements.

## 14. Interface direction

The management window is an illustrated **World Atlas and Field Manual**, not a
generic dashboard of floating rounded cards.

- Off-white paper, printed textures, stamps, taped illustrations and physical
  page tabs establish the shared frame.
- Each Saga supplies its own border art, map marks, page colors and collectible
  motifs without redesigning navigation from scratch.
- The Atlas presents only the eligible destinations, their length, Threat Band,
  completion stamps and expected rewards.
- Fighter, Supply and Mythic banners receive visibly different poster styles
  so the player always understands what kind of collectible is being pulled.
- Character pages expose family, rarity, attack behavior, encounter traits,
  Power-Up compatibility, ultimate and exact action timing.
- The Field Manual states the real formulas and uses diagrams/annotations rather
  than hiding mechanics behind vague labels.

The desktop strip remains transparent and unobtrusive. It shows the roaming
fight, World/Area/stage, wave, compact health/action indicators, menu and hide
controls. It does not show a turn order or fill the bottom of the desktop with
a solid background. Large ultimate art is brief and then gets out of the way.

## 15. Immediate build order

### Foundation

- Replace the single absolute stage route with Library/Saga/World progress.
- Support variable World lengths and Atlas Key unlocks.
- Remove the NEXT initiative display; independent action gauges are sufficient.
- Preserve the seven attack behaviors and add optional encounter traits without
  creating a type triangle.
- Add data fields for rarity, family, fighter iteration, Power-Up compatibility,
  ultimate form and Assist Page.
- Establish the illustrated World Atlas and Field Manual UI.

### Polished five-stage slice

- Mushroom Grasslands stages 1–5.
- Enough real fighters to judge the family and rarity rules.
- Four themed normal enemy families and Goomba King.
- One finished fighter banner and one Supply Spotlight.
- Several Power-Ups and Assist Pages.
- One internal transformation test.
- Atlas Rank/Threat assignment and reference-team balance tests.
- Final-quality stage pacing and transparent-strip presentation.

The old test roster remains available behind development fixtures until this
slice can replace it without breaking saves or leaving the game unplayable.

### First flagship World

- Finish Mushroom Kingdom stages 6–25, all bosses and banner milestones.
- Unlock the Atlas choice, Mastery, Mythic banners and additional Worlds.

### Second flagship World

- Finish Dream Land stages 1–25 and prove that movement, hazards, banners and
  progression are not hard-coded to Mario.

Only after this foundation is proven should production expand into the full
franchise list.

## 16. Scope control and idea intake

The size of the possible crossover is a strength only if new ideas stop
interrupting the current milestone.

New suggestions are recorded as candidates, then pass four gates before they
enter production:

1. **Distinct play:** the idea adds a kit, enemy pattern, destination or
   collectible that is not merely a name/palette swap.
2. **Asset path:** the required sprite/art is grade A/B or has a realistic
   custom-work estimate.
3. **Content home:** it belongs to a World or banner with enough surrounding
   material to justify production.
4. **Milestone fit:** it does not displace a more important dependency in the
   currently frozen build slice.

Ideas that fail a gate are parked, combined with a stronger idea or explicitly
rejected; they are not silently added to the active roadmap. Once a build slice
starts, its fighter count, enemies, systems and bosses are frozen except for
fixes discovered during playtesting.

For the Mushroom Grasslands slice, the freeze should be approximately:

- five stages and one boss;
- four normal enemy families;
- about 8–12 fighters across enough rarities to test a real banner;
- two or three Power-Ups and two or three Assist Pages;
- one Supply Spotlight and one fighter Spotlight;
- no obtainable UR yet, only an internal transformation/scale test;
- no second Saga until this slice is fun and visually coherent.

This is where future planning should deliberately cut ideas off. The Library
can remember thousands of candidates without the current build attempting all
of them.

## 17. Plan audit

### What is coherent now

- Variable 5–25-stage Worlds preserve the satisfying five-stage Area/boss
  rhythm without imposing a false 100-stage cap.
- Atlas Keys provide player choice while limiting the visible decision set to
  a manageable two to four destinations.
- Atlas Rank sets difficulty from progression rather than the exact party, so
  upgrades feel real and routes can be played in different orders.
- R can be an introductory rarity, SR has a lasting specialist purpose, SSR is
  a meaningful upgrade and UR is deliberately exceptional.
- One Mythic slot makes UR fighters, Power-Ups and future Assist Pages compete
  as different team anchors.
- Fighter iterations prevent every power-up from becoming another Mario;
  Power-Ups provide customization without requiring a new leveled unit.
- Separate Supply Tickets ensure the support/equipment gacha is used instead
  of always losing to character savings.
- Spotlight contents move into permanent Archives, retaining excitement without
  permanent loss or punishing offline time.
- The asset registry and readiness grade address the actual production
  bottleneck before rosters are promised.

### Risks that remain

- Power-Up compatibility can still create a sprite cross-product. Limit the
  first slice to authored combinations and use universal VFX where possible.
- A large fighter roster multiplies art, animation, balance and manual work.
  Release characters alongside Worlds instead of front-loading the Saga.
- Branch scaling will need simulation; Atlas Rank and Threat Bands are a model,
  not proven balance until the first reference teams can run repeatedly.
- One Mythic slot is clean but may feel overly restrictive. Keep it through the
  first UR playtest, then compare it with a cost-budget alternative.
- Too many currencies can make the manual unreadable. Start with fighter
  currency, Supply Tickets, coins and one duplicate currency; do not add more
  until a concrete sink requires them.
- Time-limited banners may be more irritating than exciting in a personal idle
  game. Begin with progress-based Spotlights and test real-time expiration later.

### Open decisions deliberately deferred

- Exact Grasslands launch roster after the sprite-sheet audit.
- Atlas Rank formula, Threat Band numbers and Mastery rewards.
- Fighter/Supply earn rates, pity and duplicate conversion prices.
- Which Mario interpretations are SSR versus UR.
- Whether the Mythic restriction remains one slot or becomes a point budget.
- Whether Area Spotlights ever use real time.

### Audit conclusion

The architecture makes sense and now accommodates the user's ideas without
requiring a fixed play order, a fixed Saga length or hundreds of immediate
assets. The plan is only feasible if the five-stage slice remains frozen and
the Atlas, Power-Up and UR systems are initially represented by the smallest
data/UI foundation needed to prove them. The next implementation should not
build the entire Library; it should remove the NEXT display, establish the new
data boundaries and make Mushroom Grasslands fun enough to justify expansion.
