# Player path and Atlas progression

Time in this document means **active runtime**: the application is open and
simulating combat. It is not a login streak, real-world deadline or promise that
unbuilt content appears by waiting. Away earnings help progression but do not
secretly clear story.

The path is a pacing target for the finished content catalog. Production still
ships one World at a time.

## 1. Opening session

### Minute 0–5: establish the desktop contract

- The first screen explains in one sentence that the game lives at the bottom
  of the desktop and can be hidden instantly.
- The player chooses compact/normal strip scale, reduced motion and sound.
- The shortcut and menu-bar restore control are shown before combat begins.
- The management window closes into a transparent practice strip.
- Mario demonstrates running and a stomp; Luigi demonstrates a ranged/bouncing
  action; the player sees gauges fill independently.

No gacha, store carousel, seven currencies or lore wall appears before the
player has seen the game move.

### Minute 5–20: form the free party

The Library prologue grants a four-person R party outside the gacha:

1. Classic Mario — mobile stomp/bruiser;
2. Classic Luigi — evasive high-jump/charge controller;
3. Toad — fast support/runner;
4. Princess Peach — backline support/controller.

These are introductory Library interpretations with era-coherent sprites; they
do not imply all four were playable in the 1985 source game. Yoshi first appears
with a World where Yoshi belongs. The title-based rule anchors environments,
enemies, bosses, releases and art direction without forbidding a crossover
party from entering them.

The first two stages teach only:

- health, action gauge and ultimate meter;
- front/back preference;
- Coins raise the single account Training Rank;
- `Advance` versus `Camp`.

### Minute 20–90: first Area and first ten-pull

- Stages 1–4 introduce one enemy behavior at a time and then combine them.
- Training Rank rises to about 5–7.
- Stage 5 is the Hammer Bros. boss.
- Victory funds the remainder of the first ten Fighter pulls and unlocks the
  Supply page with 3–5 Tickets.
- The ten-pull guarantees one non-story SR. A small three-unit focus selector
  is preferable to save deletion/rerolling.
- The newly pulled unit inherits Training Rank and can be fielded immediately.

The player has now completed the whole loop: watch → earn → understand → pull
→ change team → watch the difference.

## 2. First World: hour 1–5

The exact pace depends on how long the strip is allowed to run and how much the
owner explores management. Target:

| Active time | Expected state |
| ---: | --- |
| 1–2 h | Overworld clear; first banner and Supply choices made |
| 2–3.5 h | Open Water/High Roads; first meaningful ranged/air counterplay |
| 3–5 h | Dark World/Bowser Castle; Bowser defeated at Rank about 15–18; Atlas opens |

This is substantially slower than the prototype racing to stage 14, but the
screen is not expected to show one stage for an hour. A stage is a multi-wave
2–4-minute event; farming, failed attempts, management and idle work time form
the larger arc.

World-finale rewards:

- one Atlas Key;
- 30 Fighter pulls from first-clear rewards across the World, plus renewable
  Field Reports targeting 35–45 total pulls by hour 5;
- the Super Mario Bros. Saga Archive;
- Mastery I for the World;
- Mythic banner access;
- **CANDIDATE:** UR Super Star becomes the first Mythic option, because it can
  prove the shared Mythic slot without requiring a complete UR fighter sheet.

## 3. Atlas rules

### What the player sees

The full Library shelves are visible from the beginning as a museum-like index.
Locked Sagas show their titles, a representative silhouette, the earliest
unlock condition and whether content is actually installed. The player should
never discover at hour 190 that a favorite franchise existed all along.

The active Atlas presents only two to four eligible nodes. Each card states:

- Saga and source game;
- length and number of Areas;
- Story Threat and recommended Training Rank;
- signature fighters/Power-Ups the World can unlock;
- animation/art production state;
- prerequisite, if any;
- whether spending a Key starts real playable content now.

No Key can be consumed by a placeholder or unshipped World.

### Choice behavior

- The opening World is fixed: *Super Mario Bros.*
- A World finale may award an Atlas Key according to its data.
- One key unlocks one available World. Unchosen nodes remain available.
- Completing a node reveals new nodes but does not remove its siblings.
- Choices revealed together snapshot the same starting Story Threat.
- Internal series order can be a prerequisite where it adds meaning, but a
  player is not forced through every Mario title before seeing Kirby or Zelda.
- A short anthology cabinet can be a low-commitment route; rank prerequisites
  prevent farming only five-stage Worlds to bypass the whole Library.

### Suggested first reveal

After *Super Mario Bros.*:

1. *Super Mario Bros. 3* — continue Mario's sprite/history line;
2. *Kirby's Adventure* — begin Dream Land and prove copy/supply mechanics;
3. *Mega Man 2* — prove projectile-heavy combat and Robot Master bosses;
4. *Pac-Man* — a short Arcade Hall cabinet with maze movement/remix rules.

This selection gives continuity, a new friendly Saga, a combat-heavy Saga and a
short oddball. The recommended production route is Kirby; the player-facing
order is a choice once all four are shipped.

## 4. Access tiers, not a railroad

Atlas access uses first-cleared Areas and World prerequisites. The hours are
generous targets assuming a mixture of 10- and 25-stage Worlds.

| Access target | Earliest sensible reveal | Candidate Worlds made eligible |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Opening | 0 h | Super Mario Bros. |
| Tier A | 3–5 h / Rank 3 | Super Mario Bros. 3, Kirby's Adventure, Mega Man 2, Pac-Man |
| Tier B | 7–18 h / Rank 6 | Sonic the Hedgehog, A Link to the Past, Castlevania, Luigi's Mansion route |
| Tier C | 18–40 h / Rank 10 | Pokémon Red/Blue, Super Metroid, Street Fighter II, Dragon Quest III |
| Tier D | 30–70 h / Rank 15 | Final Fantasy VI, Fire Emblem: The Blazing Blade, Doom, EarthBound |
| Tier E | 45–100 h / Rank 20 | Minecraft, Terraria, Hollow Knight, Among Us |

“Made eligible” does not mean everything is granted or that later art is already
produced. It means the owner can spend subsequent Keys toward any shipped node
in that tier. Individual Saga sequels branch from their predecessor as well.

This structure answers the 190-hour concern: the whole proposed Library is
visible immediately, all major shelves can become selectable well before 100
active hours, and 190 hours is deep collection play—not the point where Sonic
finally appears.

## 5. Representative paths

### Historical platform path

```text
Super Mario Bros.
→ Super Mario Bros. 3
→ Kirby's Adventure
→ Sonic the Hedgehog
→ Mega Man 2
→ Super Mario World
→ A Link to the Past
```

This makes sprite evolution especially visible while still changing mechanics.

### Mario-depth path

```text
Super Mario Bros.
→ Super Mario Bros. 3
→ Super Mario World
→ Super Mario 64
→ Luigi's Mansion or Super Mario Sunshine
→ Galaxy / Odyssey / Bowser's Fury branches
```

It allows the many Mario settings the owner named without pretending they are
all stages 26–100 of one Mushroom Kingdom campaign.

### Broad crossover path

```text
Super Mario Bros.
→ Kirby's Adventure
→ Mega Man 2
→ A Link to the Past
→ Street Fighter II
→ Doom
→ Pokémon Red/Blue
→ Hollow Knight
```

This gets iconic fighters on screen early. Internal franchise sequels remain
available later.

## 6. Pacing through 190 active hours

| Active hours | Expected breadth and systems |
| ---: | --- |
| 0–1 | free party, first Area boss, first ten-pull, Supply introduced |
| 3–5 | first World finale, Atlas, Archive, Mastery and Mythic access |
| 10–25 | 2–4 World finales depending on length; 3+ Sagas visible as live choices |
| 25–50 | a stable 8–12-unit usable collection; Tier C available; first serious specialist teams |
| 50–100 | 5+ finales, all classic shelves eligible, several SSR anchors, first UR plausible |
| by 120 | one selectable unlocked UR milestone even if Mythic luck was poor |
| 100–190 | 8–15 World clears, multiple Saga branches, Mastery builds and 30–70 useful fighters if content exists |
| 190+ | collection completion, title-history routes, high Mastery and new shipped Worlds—not basic franchise access |

Roster counts are ranges because short Worlds release fewer fighters and
production cannot guarantee an unbounded catalog. “Useful” means immediately
fieldable through Training Rank, not fully starred.

## 7. Banner rhythm along a World

For any title, repeat one row per five-stage Area and use its actual finale:

| Milestone | Fighter experience | Supply experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World unlock | title banner and permanent Saga Archive visible | permanent Supply Archive |
| Stage 1 | first Area focus begins | Area Supply focus begins |
| Stage 5 | boss enters pool after victory; first ten-pull target | 3–5 initial Tickets spent |
| Intermediate Area bosses | one small release addition each, not a whole new banner UI | 5–8 Tickets per Area |
| World finale (stage 15 in SMB1) | finale focus/iteration unlock; next Spotlight grace begins | finale Page/Power-Up |
| next World finale | prior progress Spotlight archives | prior Supply focus archives |

A banner should not demand the player stop progressing to preserve it. The next
World-finale grace period normally represents another 5–10 active hours, and
all contents remain in the Archive afterward.

## 8. Daily and away behavior

The game is permitted to be closed.

- Three daily goals accumulate for up to three days rather than breaking a
  streak.
- Goals use ordinary actions: clear waves, change one loadout, use ultimates,
  fight an Area boss. No forced social/share or clock-specific task.
- Away rewards cap at 12 hours and never expire a Spotlight.
- Camp waves and repeatable Field Reports keep Starlight renewable at a target
  1.5–2.5 Fighter pulls per active hour with no daily hard cap.
- An optional “quiet day” lets the strip Camp without popups except pull-ready,
  defeat requiring attention or a completed long-term milestone.
- The return panel is one summary, not a chain of modal reward claims.

## 9. Difficulty path and recovery

Difficulty should rise through new behavior, not a smooth wall every stage.

```text
teach enemy → combine enemies → add environment → elite remix → boss exam
```

Within each Area:

- stage 1 teaches one idea;
- stage 2 adds a second enemy or positioning question;
- stage 3 creates the first uncomfortable combination;
- stage 4 is an elite/remix with a clear build hint;
- stage 5 is a boss using the Area's vocabulary.

When stuck, the player sees four honest options:

1. Camp for Coins and raise Training Rank;
2. swap row/role/target behavior using already owned units;
3. change Power-Up or Assist Page;
4. pull because the player wants more options, never because one featured unit
   is the only key.

The battle report explains which one is relevant. Farming remains a choice, not
an unexplained halt at stage 14.

## 10. UR path

UR is shown early enough to be aspirational but not handed out before the game
has a baseline.

- The first World finale, SMB1 stage 15, reveals the Mythic system and may
  unlock UR Super Star.
- A title finale can unlock a thematically complete candidate: Star Rod Kirby,
  Super Sonic, a peak boss, or a later-era fighter.
- Mythic pity persists forever across its rotations.
- Atlas milestone around 20 cleared Areas grants a selector among URs the
  account has unlocked. If content pacing makes that later than 120 active
  hours, move the milestone earlier rather than violating the time promise.
- Only one Mythic item can be active, so the first UR remains relevant as the
  collection grows.

The plan does not assume a player gets lucky to experience the system.

## 11. Adding future ideas cheaply

Every suggested title enters a parking catalog with:

```text
saga → source game → proposed length → five-stage Areas → roster hooks
→ enemy/boss set → sprite grade → prerequisite → release tier
```

Changing the preferred player route edits Atlas edges and eligibility only. It
does not renumber global stages, rebalance unrelated Worlds or move characters
between save IDs. Changing *Luigi's Mansion* from 25 to 20 stages changes that
World's length and rewards; the next World still snapshots from Atlas Rank.
