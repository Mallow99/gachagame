# Atlas and World matrix

The player chooses destinations; development still ships them in a practical
order. This document separates those two orders so a production dependency can
never masquerade as a forced campaign path.

## Progression currencies

- **Atlas Rank:** one permanent Rank per five-stage Area first-cleared. A
  15-stage World awards 3 Ranks; a five-stage cabinet awards 1.
- **Atlas Key:** unlocks one shipped World. SMB1 is free. Every World finale
  costs no additional resource and returns one Key, so a player can always
  choose another destination after completing the current one.
- **Threat snapshot:** when a choice tier first becomes available, all Worlds
  in that tier record the same starting Threat. An unchosen sibling keeps that
  snapshot and becomes an easier historical route if played much later.
- **Prerequisite:** used only where title history or a mechanic genuinely
  depends on an earlier World. Most cross-franchise choices have none.

Keys pace commitment, not ownership. Once unlocked, a World stays unlocked.
Before its first stage is cleared, the player may undo the unlock and recover
the Key. Unshipped `PLANNED` cards never accept a Key.

## World length rubric

| Length | Intended material | Normal active first-clear time |
| ---: | --- | ---: |
| 5 | one arcade cabinet, boss concept or focused side story | 1–2 h |
| 10 | short campaign with two distinct environments | 2–4 h |
| 15 | compact older title with a clean three-act arc | 3–6 h |
| 20 | mechanically rich classic with four useful chapters | 5–8 h |
| 25 | large flagship with five genuinely distinct Areas | 7–11 h |

The estimate includes party work, reports, a few retries and pulls; raw strip
combat is roughly one quarter to one third of it. Length changes by adding or
removing an entire five-stage Area, never by hiding fractional Area rewards.

## Choice tiers

The Rank requirement governs when a tier appears. Its Worlds remain available
after later tiers open.

| Tier | Rank | Initial choices | Design purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Opener | 0 | Super Mario Bros. (15, free) | teach the complete loop with familiar simple enemies |
| A | 3 | Super Mario Bros. 3 (25), Kirby's Adventure (20), Mega Man 2 (20), Pac-Man (5) | first meaningful choice: deep continuation, mobility, ranged boss gauntlet or short arcade break |
| B | 6 | Sonic the Hedgehog (15), A Link to the Past (25), Castlevania (15), Luigi's Mansion (15) | speed, adventure, deliberate melee or support/control |
| C | 10 | Pokémon Red/Blue (25), Super Metroid (25), Street Fighter II (20), Dragon Quest III (25) | collection, exploration, fighting sprites and party-RPG adaptation |
| D | 15 | Final Fantasy VI (25), Fire Emblem: Blazing Blade (25), Doom (20), EarthBound (25) | complex parties, tactics, ranged hordes and effect-heavy RPGs |
| E | 20 | Minecraft (25), Terraria pre-Hardmode (25), Hollow Knight (25), Among Us (10) | modern/custom pipelines after the engine is proven |
| F | 25 | later shipped candidates from any Saga | ongoing Library rather than a designed ending |

Rank gates are intentionally permeable. Finishing one normal Tier-A World
after SMB1 reaches Rank 7–8 and reveals Tier B. Choosing Pac-Man first reaches
Rank 4, so the player completes one more Tier-A destination before Tier B. The
short route gives quick variety, not a shortcut past most progression.

## Threat assignment

Threat is an authored encounter number, not enemy level copied from the player's
party.

```text
tierStartThreat = highest revealed tier ending Threat + 1
encounterThreat = world.threatSnapshot + localStage - 1
story enemy multiplier = 1.055 ^ (encounterThreat - 1)
boss budget multiplier = 1.00 + 0.18 per phase after the first
```

The exponential is a **TRIAL** and lives in balance data. Training, rarity,
stars and tactical matchup are simulated against it before content ships. A
World snapshot never rises because the player trained; Story therefore remains
understandable and postponed routes naturally become comfortable.

The initial tier snapshots are:

| Reveal | Starting Threat |
| --- | ---: |
| SMB1 | 1 |
| Tier A | 16 |
| Tier B | 41 |
| Tier C | 66 |
| Tier D | 96 |
| Tier E | 126 |

These generous bands prevent route-order arithmetic from colliding. During
testing, a tier may move as a block. The four choices inside it keep one start.
Branch sequels use the current highest revealed tier snapshot when they become
available, not the original prerequisite's ending Threat.

## Production order for the first releases

1. SMB1 stages 1–5 and core systems.
2. Use Yoshi/Mario 64 as engine proofs, then complete the 22-fighter Mushroom
   Archive floor before its first real pull.
3. Finish SMB1 stages 6–15, Bowser, the first Mushroom breadth batch and the
   Atlas choice screen.
4. Build Kirby's Adventure stages 1–5 and its 20-fighter Archive floor to prove
   a second visual/combat/collection grammar.
5. Finish Kirby's Adventure 6–20, the Mushroom/Dream Land breadth shelves and
   the repeatable World toolchain.
6. Build either SMB3, Mega Man 2 or Pac-Man based on asset readiness. The Atlas
   may display all three as planned, but only shipped Worlds can be entered.
7. Add the first Tier-B World after two distinct Tier-A implementations prove
   that Rank/key gates and snapshots work.

## Example player paths

**Variety-first:** SMB1 → Pac-Man → Kirby's Adventure → Castlevania → Mega Man
2 → Street Fighter II. The player sees five visual grammars quickly and returns
to longer Mario history later.

**Mario-history:** SMB1 → SMB3 → Super Mario World, with Kirby or Sonic between
them whenever desired. Source visuals visibly advance instead of mixing every
era inside the opener.

**Boss-rush:** SMB1 → Mega Man 2 → Castlevania → Kirby's Adventure → Doom. This
route stresses single-target and telegraph builds without requiring a specific
pull.

No path is canon. Reference-team tests ensure each is viable using free units,
Archive pulls and earned Supply.

## Visibility by 190 active hours

At the target 5–8 hours per normal World, 190 hours is roughly 25–30 normal
World equivalents, far beyond what an early personal build will ship. Important
characters must therefore appear through several channels before ownership:

- Atlas previews reveal the signature cast of every shipped/next-up World;
- bosses become visible in their own World within 3–11 hours of choosing it;
- Archive silhouettes name unlock conditions;
- short Worlds provide palette cleansers without consuming 10 hours;
- rerunning or completing every sibling is never required to open a new tier;
- production should alternate franchises rather than finish a 200-stage Saga.

The practical content target is that a player can encounter Mario, Kirby,
Mega Man, Pac-Man, Sonic, Link/Dracula and a fighting-game cast within the
first 50–70 active hours once those Worlds exist. Later famous candidates are
previewed honestly as planned rather than presented as obtainable.

## Atlas acceptance tests

- completing SMB1 creates one Key, Rank 3 and four equal-Threat choices;
- spending then undoing an untouched choice returns the Key;
- clearing Pac-Man raises Rank by exactly 1 and returns one Key;
- a postponed Tier-A World retains starting Threat 16;
- branch prerequisites add choices but never remove unrelated choices;
- a planned/unshipped record cannot consume a Key;
- Mastery clears do not award Atlas Rank or extra first-clear Keys;
- save/load preserves snapshots even after balance constants change.
